01 / Geography

The Land & Its Grapes

Vineyards below the Andes — Beatrice Murch, CC BY-SA 2.0 · photo details

Mendoza’s vineyards are planted across a range of elevations and growing conditions. The region’s arid climate, abundant sunshine, and irrigation systems are central to its agricultural identity.

Important grape varieties

  • Malbec — Mendoza’s best-known variety
  • Bonarda — another important red variety
  • Cabernet Sauvignon and Syrah
  • Cereza and Criolla Grande

Differences in elevation, soil, and local climate create distinct growing conditions across the province.

02 / A diverse province

Explore the Regions

Mendoza is commonly described through several major wine-growing areas. Each contributes to the province’s agricultural character.

Uco Valley

South of Mendoza city, with high-elevation vineyards, dramatic mountain views, and varied growing sites.

Primera Zona

Includes Luján de Cuyo and Maipú, areas with a long history in Mendoza’s wine industry.

Eastern & Northern areas

Important agricultural zones that add to the province’s geographic and production diversity.

Southern Mendoza

Includes San Rafael and surrounding areas, with distinctive landscapes and growing conditions.

One province, many landscapes and local identities.

A Region Shaped by Place

The Andes from Mendoza — Steve Peck, CC BY 2.0 · photo details

The Andes influence Mendoza’s climate and water supply. Snowmelt is an important source of irrigation, while elevation, soils, and temperature differences help distinguish the province’s growing areas.
